6How it works

The task
Gymnasium's LunarLander-v3. Each frame the agent sees 8 numbers (position, speed, tilt, spin, leg contacts) and fires one of 4 engines. The built-in score rewards getting closer, slower and straighter, costs fuel, and gives +100 for coming to rest or −100 for a crash. An episode is cut at 1,000 frames.
The learner: PPO
Proximal Policy Optimization flies 16 landers, collects 16,384 transitions, then nudges the network a small, clipped step towards actions that did better than the critic expected. Small steps keep one lucky stretch from wrecking the policy.
The network
Two separate small networks. The actor (8 → 64 → 64 → 4, tanh) outputs a probability per engine. The critic (8 → 64 → 64 → 1) estimates V(s), the score still to come.
Reward hacking
Adding “+1 for every upright and slow frame” pays for being in a state. Hovering until the 1,000-frame limit then earns about +800, against about +100 for landing (which ends the payments). The agent learns to hover. Its critic values hovering at about 735, while the real score stays near 0.
The fix
Pay for improvement instead: F = γ·Φ(s′) − Φ(s). Over an episode these terms cancel out except for the first and last, so there is nothing to farm, and Ng, Harada and Russell (1999) proved this never changes which policy is optimal.
Fair testing
Every model is tested on the plain score, with the most likely action, on the same 50 fixed landing sites (seeds 10,000+, never used in training), and a verdict needs 3 training seeds.
